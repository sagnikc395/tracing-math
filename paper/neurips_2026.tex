\workshoptitle{Interpretability for Discovery}

\begin{abstract}
We audit whether hidden-state probes identify when mathematical reasoning first becomes invalid, or
instead exploit easier correlates of an invalid prefix. In a case study of \model{} on \dataset{},
a linear probe predicts the persistent target \emph{invalid so far} with held-out AUROC $0.866$.
This number overstates what the representation result establishes: prefix TF--IDF reaches $0.760$,
structural metadata reaches $0.780$, and metadata augmented with final-answer correctness reaches
$0.857$; the last AUROC is not distinguishable from the hidden-state result under a paired
whole-trace bootstrap (difference $0.009$, 95\% CI $[-0.014,0.032]$). Hidden states retain a larger
advantage on trace-level localization (Process F1 $0.393$ versus $0.288$ for metadata plus outcome;
paired difference $0.105$ [$0.047,0.163]$), but localize the exact annotated boundary on only
$28.9\%$ of erroneous traces and falsely alarm on $38.8\%$ of correct traces. A post-hoc,
metadata-matched transition probe obtains AUROC $0.769$ on held-out pairs, suggesting that an
onset-related state change is decodable but not providing confirmatory evidence. Two verdict
readouts fail their behavioral validity checks, so the intervention remains a failed pilot. The
supported conclusion is a model-specific gap among prefix decodability, onset localization,
calibration, and demonstrated causal use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Process supervision asks whether each step of a solution is valid, rather than whether the final
answer happens to be correct \citep{cobbe2021verifiers,lightman2023verify}. A related mechanistic
question is whether a solving model's own representation changes when its reasoning first goes
wrong. Probe accuracy alone cannot answer that question. If every boundary after the first error
is labeled positive, a classifier may recognize accumulated inconsistency, solution length,
generator style, or final-answer outcome without responding at the onset.

We separate four claims that are often collapsed: (i) invalid prefixes are linearly decodable;
(ii) a score localizes the first error; (iii) the signal transfers across benchmark sources; and
(iv) the model behaviorally uses the decoded direction. We test these claims in one explicitly
bounded setting: \model{} on \dataset{} \citep{yang2024qwenmath,zheng2025processbench}. The primary
pipeline uses problem-grouped splits and a persistent invalid-so-far target. We then audit it with
prefix-text and metadata controls, equal-trace weighting, trace-macro metrics, source and generator
stratification, an onset-aligned analysis, and a matched transition probe.

The audit changes the interpretation. The hidden-state probe's pooled AUROC is high, but a
diagnostic model that is given structural metadata and final-answer correctness nearly matches it.
The hidden state is more useful for the thresholded trace-level task, and its score changes near the
human annotation, but localization remains unreliable. The causal assay is not interpretable
because its baseline verdict scores rank valid and invalid prefixes below chance. We therefore
make one narrow contribution: a worked example showing that strong prefix decoding and
cross-source ranking can coexist with weak onset localization, shortcut sensitivity, poor
calibration, and no validated causal evidence.

\section{Related work and contribution boundary}

External process reward models learn step quality from human or model supervision
\citep{lightman2023verify,li2023stepaware}. \dataset{} annotates the earliest error in 3,400
model-generated traces from GSM8K, MATH, OlympiadBench, and Omni-MATH
\citep{zheng2025processbench,hendrycks2021math,he2024olympiadbench,gao2024omnimath}. We repartition
these benchmark sources for representation analysis; they are heterogeneous sources, not
independent mathematical domains or evidence of model-family generalization.

Closest to this work, Sun et al. probe arithmetic errors and use the probe for selective
re-prompting; ReProbe learns hidden-state step scorers for test-time scaling; and STEP uses
hidden-state scores to prune reasoning traces
\citep{sun2025arithmetic,ni2026reprobe,liang2026earlysignals}. Table~\ref{tab:delta} states the
comparison at the level needed for our claim. Our novelty is not that hidden states predict
reasoning quality. It is the joint audit of a human first-error target using first-crossing,
matched-transition, source-transfer, shortcut, and failed-assay evidence.

Probing work cautions that decodability need not imply behavioral use
\citep{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing,elazar2021amnesic}. This distinction is especially
important here: an additive intervention is evidence only if its behavioral readout first
distinguishes the relevant baseline states.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Data flow, model, and split}

Each \dataset{} trace supplies a problem, generator identity, written steps, source, final-answer
correctness, and a human first-error index $e_i$, with $e_i=-1$ for fully correct traces. Traces
sharing normalized problem text form a group. Seed 42 assigns groups within source $\times$
error-status strata to 60\% train, 20\% validation, and 20\% test. A 2,048-token limit excludes a
complete trace rather than truncating it. Table~\ref{tab:dataflow} gives the full split accounting;
Appendix~\ref{app:data} gives source-level counts.

We run \model{} in bfloat16 and record its 1,536-dimensional residual-stream state at each written
step and each of 29 hidden-state indices. The completed extraction used batch size 16 on one NVIDIA
A100 GPU. Python 3.10+, PyTorch 2.2--2.x, Transformers 4.45--4.x, and scikit-learn 1.4--1.x are
specified in the accompanying environment. Artifact identity fixes the model, context length,
dtype, and dataset SHA-256 (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Persistent target, probe, and trace-level estimands}

At boundary $k$ of trace $i$, the frozen primary target is
\begin{equation}
y_{ik}=\mathbb{1}[e_i\geq 0\ \wedge\ k\geq e_i].
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
It means \emph{invalid so far}; it does not label only the first invalid step. A class-balanced,
L2 logistic probe is fit separately at each index after train-set standardization. Validation AUROC
selects $C\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10\}$; validation Process F1 selects a threshold on a 0.005 grid from
0.05 to 0.95 and then the layer. The selected index is 23, $C=0.01$, threshold $0.645$; it is refit
on train plus validation and evaluated once on test.

The predicted first error is the first threshold crossing, or $-1$ if none occurs. We report
boundary AUROC and average precision, exact error-boundary accuracy among erroneous traces,
correct-trace rejection, their harmonic mean (Process F1), complete-trace accuracy, and detection
within one or two steps. These localization metrics weight traces equally. Primary intervals use
1,000 whole-trace bootstrap draws; the post-hoc paired comparisons use 2,000 draws because they
were added in a later audit configuration. We state this difference rather than presenting the
two draw counts as one frozen protocol.

\subsection{Shortcut and weighting audits}

Every added control uses the same train/validation/test groups and selects its threshold on
validation only. Current-step TF--IDF uses the destination step. Prefix TF--IDF uses the problem
and all reasoning text available through the current boundary. Structural metadata uses source,
generator, absolute and fractional step position, trace length, and token count. A deliberately
strong diagnostic adds final-answer correctness; because that field can be unavailable online, it
is a shortcut audit rather than a deployable baseline. A shuffled-label hidden probe checks the
fitting procedure. We report paired probe-minus-control intervals from trace resampling.

The primary objective and boundary AUROC weight each boundary equally. To expose its consequence,
we recompute AUROC, average precision, and step F1 using weights inverse to the number of boundaries
in each trace. We also stratify trace-level outcomes by source, generator (at least 20 test traces),
final-answer correctness, error position, trace-length quartile, and token-count quartile. These
subgroups are descriptive, not multiple confirmatory tests.

\subsection{Post-hoc onset analyses}

The following analyses were designed after the primary test result and are labeled post-hoc. We
circularly shift each frozen score trajectory 5,000 times, preserving its values while breaking
alignment to the annotation. We compare the score jump at each noninitial error with a transition
from a fully correct trace, preferring the same source and generator and then minimizing differences
in relative position, trace length, and token count.

The transition probe is a separate analysis of $h_{i,e_i}-h_{i,e_i-1}$. Every eligible erroneous
transition is paired with a correct-trace transition in the same frozen partition. The resulting
balanced sets contain 1,152 train, 387 validation, and 380 test pairs. Layer and $C$ are selected
by validation AUROC; index 21 and $C=0.01$ are selected. The test report includes AUROC, average
precision, within-pair accuracy, and a two-way bootstrap over error IDs and reused correct-trace IDs.
Controls predict from position, destination-step TF--IDF, or shuffled labels. This protocol is now
fully specified and executable, but the test set was not untouched when the analysis was conceived.

\section{Results}

\subsection{High prefix AUROC is not specific to hidden-state invalidity}

The selected probe reaches AUROC $0.866$ (95\% CI $[0.849,0.884]$), average precision $0.843$
($[0.813,0.870]$), and step F1 $0.743$ ($[0.716,0.769]$). Within erroneous traces, first-step
centering leaves pooled AUROC at $0.881$ ($[0.862,0.900]$), and mean per-trace AUROC is $0.968$
($[0.958,0.976]$). Stable trace offsets therefore do not explain all discrimination.

The shortcut audit nevertheless makes the pooled result much less specific. Table~\ref{tab:controls}
shows that structural metadata reaches AUROC $0.780$, prefix text reaches $0.760$, and metadata plus
final outcome reaches $0.857$. The hidden-minus-outcome-metadata AUROC difference is only $0.009$
($[-0.014,0.032]$), while the Process F1 difference is $0.105$ ($[0.047,0.163]$). Thus hidden states
add trace-level localization value under the selected threshold, but pooled ranking alone cannot be
interpreted as an isolated representation of mathematical invalidity.

Equal trace weighting changes AUROC by $+0.001$ (95\% paired CI $[-0.007,0.010]$), average precision

from $0.298$ to $0.512$, and generator-level estimates are correspondingly heterogeneous
(Appendix~\ref{app:subgroups}).

\subsection{First-error localization remains weak}

The hidden score localizes $28.9\%$ of erroneous traces exactly, $59.3\%$ within one step, and
$72.7\%$ within two. It crosses somewhere on $88.7\%$ of erroneous traces, but crosses on
$38.8\%$ of fully correct traces. Among detections, the signed error averages $+0.59$ steps
($[+0.36,+0.80]$), indicating late detection. Process F1 is $0.393$ ($[0.351,0.436]$) and
complete-trace accuracy is $0.404$.

Post-hoc temporal tests show more alignment than these threshold metrics alone suggest. Exact
localization is $0.289$ versus $0.164$ under circular shifts ($p=0.0002$). Across 380 noninitial
errors, the mean onset jump is $0.257$; matched correct transitions rise $0.113$, for a paired
difference of $0.144$ ($[0.096,0.193]$). The mean score continues from $0.341$ immediately before
the error to $0.559$ at onset, $0.680$ one step later, and $0.731$ two steps later. This is a
gradual transition into a persistent state, not a sharp alarm.

The matched transition probe reaches AUROC $0.769$ ($[0.713,0.820]$), average precision $0.741$
($[0.672,0.816]$), and paired accuracy $0.753$ ($[0.676,0.824]$). Position, destination-step
TF--IDF, and shuffled-label controls reach AUROC $0.63$, $0.67$, and $0.58$. This result supports
linear decodability of an onset-related change on the already-inspected split. Replication on an
untouched set or second model is required before treating it as confirmatory.

\subsection{Source ranking transfers; model generalization is untested}

Separate probes trained on one ProcessBench source and tested on another have mean off-diagonal
AUROC $0.805$ versus $0.845$ on the diagonal; every off-diagonal cell exceeds $0.739$. Thresholded
Process F1 is less stable: means are $0.261$ off diagonal and $0.336$ on diagonal, with individual
off-diagonal values from $0.055$ to $0.371$. These cells show cross-source ranking within one
benchmark construction. They do not establish transfer to another model size, family, or dataset.

\subsection{The causal experiment is a failed pilot}

The original \texttt{CORRECT}/\texttt{INCORRECT} readout has baseline AUROC $0.283$ and specificity
zero. A revised question with conditional Yes/No probabilities remains below chance (AUROC $0.342$,
specificity $0.203$ on 256 balanced boundaries). The intervention code now requires both AUROC
$>0.5$ and nonzero specificity before a learned-direction effect is interpreted. Neither readout
passes. Random orthogonal directions cannot substitute for a positive control of assay
sensitivity, so the dose response is not causal evidence for or against behavioral use. We report
the pilot only to delimit the evidence and do not include it in the main contribution.

\section{Limitations, responsible use, and conclusion}

This is a one-model, one-benchmark case study. We did not run the requested second-family or
second-scale replication, so the title and claims name \model{}. The transition probe, temporal
randomization, prefix and metadata controls, trace-weighting analysis, and subgroup analyses are
post-hoc. No dated public preregistration exists; the repository records the frozen split seed,
target, model-selection grid, and original bootstrap count, but cannot retroactively establish
preregistration. Corrected/error twins have not received independent human verification, so no
counterfactual-patching result is reported. A stronger next experiment would verify such twins,
hold problem and boundary fixed, reserve an untouched onset test set, and validate the behavioral
readout with a known effective intervention before testing a probe direction.

The detector is unsuitable for grading, certification, or unattended feedback. Its $38.8\%$
false-alarm rate on correct traces could penalize valid work, and its late detections could allow an
invalid derivation to propagate. The source, generator, outcome, and length sensitivity makes
deployment shift especially risky. Positive uses are limited to research diagnostics with human
or formal verification of consequential mathematical claims.

The central empirical finding is a gap, not an internal ``knowledge'' claim. In \model{},
invalid-so-far prefixes are linearly decodable and their scores change near annotated errors. Yet
nuisance metadata nearly matches pooled AUROC, exact onset localization is low, calibration varies
across trace length and source, cross-model generalization is unknown, and no behavioral assay
supports a causal conclusion. Reporting all of these together gives a more auditable account of
what hidden-state verifier scores do and do not establish.
